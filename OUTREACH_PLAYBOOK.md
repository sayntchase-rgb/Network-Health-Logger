# Network Health Diagnostic — Outreach Playbook

## Objective

Get the first paid **$75 Network Health Diagnostic** before adding more software features.

The purpose of outreach is not to sell software. It is to find businesses that already experience intermittent internet or connectivity problems and offer a small diagnostic that produces evidence.

## Ideal customer profile

Prioritize small businesses where unreliable internet can interrupt:

- online payments
- cloud software
- appointment booking
- VoIP or customer communications
- printing or file transfer
- customer records
- connected cameras or devices
- remote or multi-user office work

Good first categories:

1. Accounting and tax offices
2. Insurance agencies
3. Dental and small professional offices
4. Printing, media, and creative shops
5. Auto repair shops using connected systems
6. Appointment-based salons and barber shops
7. Coworking or shared-office operators

## Qualification questions

Use a short conversation. Do not start with a technical pitch.

Ask:

- Do you ever have internet drops, slow periods, or systems that randomly disconnect?
- Does it affect payments, bookings, calls, cloud software, cameras, or staff work?
- Is the problem intermittent enough that it is difficult to show your ISP or IT support?
- Would a short monitoring period and written report be useful before replacing equipment or escalating support?

If the answer is no, move on. Do not force the sale.

## 20-prospect outreach target

Track:

- business name
- category
- contact method
- date contacted
- matching problem: yes / no / unknown
- interest: no / maybe / demo / paid
- objection
- next action

Initial target:

- 20 prospects contacted
- 5 conversations about a real connectivity problem
- 2 demonstrations
- 1 paid diagnostic

## Phone / in-person opener

> Hi, I’m testing a small network-diagnostic service for local businesses. I’m not selling internet service. I monitor connectivity and latency over time so intermittent drops or slow periods can be documented instead of guessed at. Do you ever have internet problems that disappear before support can see them?

If yes:

> I’m offering the first diagnostics at $75. I collect observations over an agreed period and give you a written report showing uptime, latency, failed checks, outage events, and practical next troubleshooting steps. It can give you evidence before replacing equipment or calling the ISP again.

## Demo flow

Keep the demonstration under five minutes:

1. Show the synthetic demo HTML report.
2. Point out the monitoring period and target.
3. Show uptime and latency.
4. Show degraded / down checks and outage events.
5. Show plain-language findings and recommendations.
6. Explain that the report separates observed evidence from possible causes.
7. Ask whether they have a similar intermittent problem.

Do not spend the demo explaining Python, GitHub, or the internal code unless the prospect specifically asks.

## Objection handling

### “My internet works fine right now.”

That is exactly why intermittent problems are difficult to diagnose. The service is useful when the issue happens unpredictably and is gone by the time someone checks it.

### “My ISP can test it.”

They can, and this is not a replacement for ISP support. The diagnostic gives you a time-based record you can use when escalating an intermittent issue.

### “Can you fix the router too?”

The $75 offer is the diagnostic and report. Equipment changes or deeper troubleshooting should be scoped separately after the evidence is reviewed.

### “Can you guarantee the cause?”

No. The service documents observed connectivity behavior and suggests next diagnostic steps. It does not guarantee root-cause identification.

### “Why should I pay for ping tests?”

The value is not a single ping. The value is collecting observations over time, organizing the history, summarizing outages and latency behavior, and turning it into a report that supports a troubleshooting decision.

## Rules for the first 10 conversations

- Do not discount below $75 automatically.
- Do not promise root-cause identification.
- Do not add requested features before understanding whether they repeat across customers.
- Record exact objections.
- Ask every completed customer whether the report was useful and what was missing.
- Ask satisfied customers for one referral or permission to create an anonymized case study.

## Decision gate

After 20 prospects:

- **If nobody has the problem:** change the target market before changing the software.
- **If people have the problem but will not pay:** investigate positioning, urgency, and pricing.
- **If people pay but request the same missing capability:** build that capability next.
- **If customers want ongoing monitoring:** test a monthly managed-monitoring offer.

Do not build recurring monitoring until customer conversations justify it.
